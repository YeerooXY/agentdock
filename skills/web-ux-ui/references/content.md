# Concrete wording and evidence

Owner correction, 2 November 2026: avoid decorative em dashes and middle-dot separators in headings, status text and metadata. Use spacing or ordinary sentences. Remove atmospheric footer jokes, congratulatory empty-state filler and redundant filter subtitles. Put secondary storage explanations behind a named details control. Check empty, completed and error states, not only the initial screen.

Keep development status in the handoff. Do not call the website a demo, prototype or sample site, and do not add development-status banners. Explain real limitations beside the affected action without inventing availability: a download needs an actual artifact and a form must not claim delivery without a receiver. Preserve necessary prices, terms, privacy disclosures and error recovery.

Write from verified product facts and the visitor's next decision. Use concrete names, actions and consequences. Preserve prices, units, eligibility, limitations, privacy information and recovery instructions.

Replace interchangeable promises with the actual behavior:

| Weak draft | Useful direction |
| --- | --- |
| Unlock your potential | Name what the person can do with this product |
| A seamless workflow | Describe which steps or records are connected |
| More than a platform | State the product category and specific job |
| Get started | Name the real next step: Download for Windows, Check availability, Create project |
| Something went wrong | Explain the known failure and a usable retry or correction |

Do not infer AI authorship from a phrase. Fix the content problem: vagueness, missing facts, repetition or misleading action labels. Do not replace one generic slogan with another. When the project lists prohibited wording, report each occurrence with its location and an example; fix within scope and flag unrelated occurrences.

Remove small explanatory text that repeats a heading or an obvious control. Remove repeated workflow summaries when the product demonstration already communicates them. Use plain platform names such as Windows and Android; add a requirement only where it changes the decision. Keep prices, limitations and recovery steps visible. Put screenshot provenance or secondary explanation in an appropriate detail view instead of repeating a caption beneath every screen.

Avoid slogan-like sequences of short commands, especially rhetorical triplets such as "Read your consoles. Send a message. Open your files." Use an ordinary sentence describing the relevant behavior, for example: "You can check a running session and reply from your phone." Keep the tone natural and factual; remove clever-sounding transitions, exaggerated confidence and decorative rhythm. This does not prohibit useful factual lists or necessary sequential instructions.

## Page copy workflow

1. Establish the real offer, audience, primary action and constraints from the project. Mark missing proof instead of inventing it.
2. Draft headings that describe each section's job. Remove sections that add no decision-relevant information.
3. Put consequences near the action: cost, recurring terms, platform availability, delivery conditions, what is submitted or downloaded.
4. Use links for destinations and buttons for actions. A CTA opening a selection step should describe that step. Don't label a lead form as an instant download.
5. Read every sentence over 20 words for unnecessary clauses. Check short copy for contrast reveals, self-answered promotional questions, decorative uppercase labels and repeated reassurances.
6. Test the actual wording in narrow layouts and error states. Keep meaningful labels visible; don't rely on placeholder text alone.

Never invent testimonials, customers, usage numbers, guarantees, certifications or conversion results. Clearly identify illustrative examples and keep them separate from operational status or live social proof. Use competitor content to study specificity and information sequence, not to copy prose or treat its performance as known.

Sources: [GOV.UK writing guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/content-design/writing-for-gov-uk), [Apple writing guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing), [NN/G usability of concise scannable text](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/). Apply the user's voice and current project canon first.

## CTA playbook by site type

These are working defaults informed by the 31 October 2026 reference study: 36 selected URLs, desktop Chromium and emulated iPhone WebKit captures, plus 12 public first-click checks. Canva and paper republic were excluded from design evidence because the captures showed blocked/unsupported content and Google respectively. Other captures include consent overlays, missing media and incomplete scroll sampling. Use the [study register](cta-study.md) to locate examples and limitations. No conversion outcomes were measured.

### Local food businesses

Establish what is sold, where to visit, when the business is open and the actual ordering channel. Choose the main action from the operation: directions, browse menu, call, reserve or pre-order. Keep today's useful visit facts near that action. Put a product/menu preview before a lengthy brand story when the visitor needs to choose a product. Repeat the same useful action after relevant details, preserving location and collection context.

Observed examples: Espresso Embassy puts hours, contact and its no-reservations policy in a compact page. Artizán's cake-order link leads to a priced catalogue. Mazel Tov repeats reservation links in navigation, hero and venue information; its first click opens an external party/date/time selection screen. These support different tasks; do not invent booking for a walk-in-only cafe or label a catalogue link as a completed order. Long stories on Arán and Joseph Brot demonstrate a tradeoff between editorial presentation and access to visit information.

Test: can a visitor find the correct location and opening time, understand pickup/booking conditions and reach the right destination on a phone?

### Appointment and class booking

Keep service, location, duration and known price/conditions attached to the booking route. If the next state asks for a location, service or availability, name that step. Distinguish a telephone booking from an online reservation. Repeat booking after the relevant service or staff information; a persistent action is useful only when it preserves context and does not hide content or keyboard focus.

Observed examples: Close's header link jumps to a lower booking area and the online link opens a Salonic location screen. H'air's Book now leads to a page explaining telephone booking. Cut Throat's identical booking labels belong to barber, restaurant and event services. Frame separates class and studio discovery; Ruffians' newsletter overlay competes with a small booking control. Copy the service distinction, not the interruption or ambiguous labels.

Test: can a visitor choose the right service/location, distinguish inquiry from confirmed booking, and find cancellation or deposit terms before commitment?

### Free software downloads

Show the product's actual job, a real screenshot when available, supported operating systems and a concrete download action. Put OS, version, architecture or file information beside the action when it changes the choice. Retain an explicit platform selector even when detection is available. For a desktop-only product viewed on a phone, explain availability and offer a useful way to find the desktop download later; do not suggest a nonexistent phone app.

Observed examples: Anki's first download action jumps to platform choices rather than transferring a file. VLC groups the main download with version/platform details. Todoist changes its hero to an iPhone App Store route and retains alternatives; its Android link opens the Android download page. Obsidian's emulated iPhone capture shows a macOS headline download, a reason to test detection rather than copy it. Zotero separates the desktop app, browser connector and mobile artifacts. Donation, signup and downloading are different actions.

Test: verify the exact artifact/destination, OS match and recovery from a missing download. Show any account requirement and distinguish a free app from paid optional services.

### SaaS free tiers and pricing

Keep price, billing period, per-seat/unit meaning, limits and trial/renewal terms in the same decision block as the plan action. Maintain the selected plan through signup. A free tier, a timed trial and a paid subscription need different explanations. Preserve comparable information when cards stack on a phone; a long feature table is secondary to the first plan choice.

Observed examples: Notion and Trello place free and paid options side by side on desktop, then stack them on phones. Their inspected free-plan actions lead to account forms; completion and post-signup plan retention were not tested. Figma exposes plan and seat choices. Calendly repeats similar action labels across different plans. Dropbox Basic pairs signup with a stated storage limit and repeats it after product explanations. Treat identical labels with different plan destinations as separate actions.

Test: ask a reader to identify what is free, what they will pay, when they will pay it and which limitations apply. Verify the selected plan and billing term after the first click and after back navigation.

### Specialist shops

Lead into useful product categories or a specific item. Pair product identity and price with the action, then expose variants, stock, delivery market/cost and returns before commitment. Resolve country/currency when it changes the offer. Keep optional newsletter promotions from obscuring discovery or refusal. Add a sticky purchase action only when selection, price and keyboard/touch behaviour remain clear.

Observed examples: AeroPress's Shop Now opens a coffee-maker collection; product demonstrations and named collection links repeat useful shopping routes. Present & Correct's New opens a catalogue with prices, availability and sorting. Papier's Start shopping initially confirms the shipping market. KeepCup's phone view has overlapping country and consent dialogs. Neither market selection nor signup is an add-to-cart action. Review counts and discount claims in these examples were not independently substantiated.

Test: can a visitor choose the correct variant, see total/recurring costs and delivery terms, edit the selection and recover from unavailable stock? First-click observations do not establish checkout completion.

### Large catalogues and marketplaces

Use search, category structure and location/date context where those are prerequisites to relevant results. A cart, login or social-provider button may be prominent without being the primary visitor task. Carry product, destination, dates, occupancy or delivery context through subsequent states. Repeated action labels need grouping by destination and intent, not by label alone.

Observed examples: IKEA exposes search and categories; its inspected storage link opens a category page. Apple's model-specific buy links lead to different configurations; the inspected destination offers colour and capacity choices. Wolt's captured login overlay caused the automatic detector to choose Facebook; its merchant-discovery task remains separate. Booking.com's useful starting point is destination/date/party search, despite a competing loyalty-signup overlay. Do not impose this complexity on a small local site.

Test: can a visitor reach relevant results, compare the correct items and retain their context? Availability, delivery costs and urgency remain unverified until checked against the actual selected conditions.

### Honest persuasion and evidence

Use a concrete offer, accurate terms, authentic supporting material and an appropriately small first step. Repeat an action after information that helps the decision. Measure task completion, mistakes, understanding of costs and abandonment points; click counts alone do not establish a useful improvement.

Do not use invented deadlines or scarcity, fabricated reviews, hidden recurring costs, default paid extras or guilt-based refusal. A screenshot of urgency does not prove it is false. Distinguish a prohibited design choice from a verified legal violation. False limited-time claims are addressed by the UCPD; additional payments require express consent under the Consumer Rights Directive. The DSA's interface provisions concern covered online platforms and have scope/exemption rules; they are not a universal label for every business website. Check current primary sources and national implementation before assessing a particular site. See sources.
