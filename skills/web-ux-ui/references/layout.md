# First screen and arrangement

The first screen should establish identity, purpose, a useful next action and enough evidence to orient the visitor. Priorities depend on the task:

- Marketing: a concrete job or offer, short explanation, accurate action and a legible product/work example.
- Shop: range, search and useful categories. Catalogue: query/category, comparable items and relevant filters. Product: identity, gallery, price, selected variant and purchase context.
- Application: current work, relevant status and actions. Returning users usually need their records rather than a repeated promotional introduction.
- Booking: service, location/date constraints, availability and cost basis.

The fold varies with viewport, browser bars, zoom and overlays. Put orientation and the next action early; allow detailed evaluation below. Do not shrink type to fit an arbitrary height.

## Useful compositions

**Introduction plus demonstration:** group headline, explanation and action; place a useful demonstration alongside or below when it needs width. On phones, use a legible relevant crop or a simpler truthful example, with access to the full view where useful.

**Search plus comparable results:** keep query and active filters visible, show result context, maintain comparable information and preserve scroll/filter state when returning from a detail page.

**Gallery plus decision panel:** keep variant, total/cost context, availability and action together. Use imagery to answer scale, fit, material and compatibility questions. Sticky content must leave reading and keyboard focus usable.

**Application workspace:** group controls by task, distinguish navigation from record actions, retain context across views and offer keyboard alternatives to dragging. Dense layouts can be appropriate for comparison and repeated work.

Use real content before tuning spacing. Check long headings, translations, empty lists and unusually large values. Breakpoints follow the content's constraints. Reorder deliberately on narrow screens; keep meaningful reading and focus order aligned.

Avoid decorative cards around every sentence, interchangeable sections, hover-only discovery, nested scrolling without purpose, and tiny full-desktop screenshots used as explanations.

Sources: [NN/G homepage principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/), [scrolling and attention](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/), [WCAG reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html). These are conditional design patterns, not a universal winning layout.
